## Process

- Work is done as supervisor-gated work packages (`~/.claude/skills/{work-breakdown,scope-of-work,
  work-execution,work-acceptance}`; decision loop in `~/.claude/skills/CLAUDE.md`). Ordinary small
  requests from the supervisor in chat are fine without a package.
- Interface contract between the technical and presentation sides: `work-packages/interface-contract.md`
  (manifest `work-packages/interface-contract.toml`); amend it, never drift from it.
- Approvals and acceptances are verbal for now and are quoted verbatim in `work-packages/WPn-decisions.md`.
- **No AI attribution:** no `Co-Authored-By` trailers or "generated by" lines on commits, PRs or documents.
- Ask the supervisor only problem-level questions, one at a time, with plain options.
- Repository, history, index (the supervisor, 2026-11-15): the repository's job is the current state; git history's
  job is the historical trajectory; indexes and ledgers in the tree (`work-packages/INDEX.md`, `driver-log.md`, the
  live decisions files, the length ledger, the claims register) make the historical material discoverable and
  dereferenceable on demand (`git show <hash>:<path>`). A package's documents, spike outputs and notes leave the
  tree at its landing, recorded in the index with the commits where its scope was locked and its results accepted.

  merged by a code owner, who may be its author; a release PR into `main` is approved by a code owner other than
  its author; a paper PR is merged by a paper approver other than its author.

## Vocabulary and mathematics

- configuration c (design variables) → attributes a (evaluation outcomes) → metrics m (conditioned
  attributes, strictly positive, larger-is-better). The package covers identification (fit) and
  analysis; conditioning is the analyst's job with scikit-learn tools.
- The model is a posynomial frontier; K = 1 (monomial) is built; K > 1 and kernel methods are
  documented extensions (GGP-compatible only).
- β is the log-mean-exp (entropic) soft maximum, never the unnormalized log-sum-exp.
- Arbitrary dimension d: arrays and indexed symbols; never hard-code x, y, z or a, b, c.

## scikit-learn conformance (requirement)

Follow `notes/sklearn-conformance.md`: `scikit-learn>=1.8`; immutable-default
params stored verbatim and validated in `fit`; `validate_data` (`reset=True` in fit, `reset=False`
elsewhere after `check_is_fitted`); tags only via `__sklearn_tags__`; zeros tolerated and negatives
rejected with "Negative values in data"; decomposition-family semantics (`transform`,
`inverse_transform`, `score_samples`; no `predict` on the transformer); `parametrize_with_checks`
with `xfail_strict=True` and a reasoned (ideally empty) expected-failures dict; numpydoc docstrings
with doctests.

## Derivations and docs

- Load-bearing results get plain-SymPy lemma scripts in `derivation/` (hc-marimo pattern,
  symproof-compatible ledger; see `notes/proof-conventions.md`).
- Docs are Markdown + LaTeX, discursive, each claim citing its lemma; no reference enters
  `docs/references.bib` without a resolved DOI or stable URL.
- **No metanarration (standing rule, the supervisor, 2026-11-14):** no sentence whose subject is the text, the
  page, the figure's placement or the reader's experience ("You have now seen the same table three ways", "this
  page names", "the figure further down", "as shown above", "in our own account"). Say the thing. The residual
  checker (`scripts/check_residuals.py`, kind `metanarration`) flags the openers; a reviewer strikes the rest.
- Vocabulary: `glossary.yaml` is the one source of terms. One object, one name; use the glossary's
  term, replace jargon that does no work with plain words. Check with
  `uv run python scripts/check_vocabulary.py` and the reading in
  `notes/vocabulary-review.md`.
- Claims: every claim a reader can rely on is backed (a lemma step, a generated value, a test, a
  verified source) or is narrowed or dropped; no caveats added to prove it. Register and mechanism:
  `notes/claims-review.md`. Keep the model, the fit and the analysis apart in prose.
- Negative edits are welcome, even preferred: the marginal-value test (does this earn its place) applies to every
  piece of content, prose, figures, tables, code and notes, and it is applied to what is already there, not only to
  additions; asking it only of additions still grows without bound. A unit that deletes is a good unit; each
  deletion states what the content no longer did.
- Reviews run the thing: a reviewer of any delivery runs the code, analyses the data it produces, and looks at the
  rendered result (plots, notebooks, docs pages, the paper) in a browser or as an image, with screenshots in the
  record; reading code and prose alone is not a review. A stderr line on a rendered page, a plot that does not say
  what its caption says, a page that does not build: these are found by looking, not by reading.
- Figures: every figure earns its place or goes; judged as rendered by a visual loop (cold read,
  the sentence test, ink as data, legibility, shows and does not show):
  `notes/figure-review.md`.
- Bigger candidate edits (a new result, a model change, a figure from new data) run six stages, each named in the
  scope: research spike, decide, build, quality control, integrate, review the cascade to a clean merged state
  (`work-packages/PM-BRIEF.md`, "Bigger candidate edits run the full lifecycle").
- Site framing: Tall's three worlds, glass-box seams, machine verification vs human validation
  (`notes/presentation-framing.md`).
